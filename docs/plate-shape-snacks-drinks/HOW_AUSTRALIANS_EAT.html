How Australians actually eat, and where the planner differs

Your questions from 2026-11-19, after the m5-04 snack design: does a snack tab repeat what the meals already do with fruit, and what is the planner missing compared with what people really eat?

Research from the ABS national nutrition surveys (2011–12 and 2023), compared with 300 live plates from our planner and a search of the 3,741-food catalogue · 2026-11-19

The short answer The foods are mostly there. What's wrong is the shape of the plate. Our catalogue already has burgers, sandwiches, wraps, kebabs, pizza, sushi, curries and stir-fries. They already make up 40% of the lunch and dinner plates. But the planner treats a burger like a plain chicken breast that still needs two sides, and it picks those sides by food group alone. That gives you "Hamburger + frozen strawberries + protein yoghurt" and "Pizza + bamboo shoots + frozen banana". Nobody eats that. In real life a burger comes with a coffee.

1What Australians eat at each meal

Breakfast · the smallest meal

  • Cereal with milk is about half of all adult breakfasts.
  • Bread or toast with a spread is about 40%.
  • A cooked breakfast (eggs, bacon) is only about 11%. Bacon, egg and toast is the most common one, eaten by 3.5% of adults.
  • Meat appears in fewer than 10% of breakfasts.
  • Tea or coffee is the most popular breakfast drink.
NNPAS 2011–12 (Deakin, Leech et al.; Sui et al. 2017)

Lunch · the middle meal

  • Sandwiches, rolls and wraps with chicken, beef, ham or canned fish are the most popular lunches with meat.
  • Hot meat meals are uncommon at lunch.
  • Ham is the most common processed meat.
  • Lunch is mostly grains, salad vegetables and a drink, which is often a soft drink.
NNPAS 2011–12 (Sui, Raubenheimer & Rangan 2017)

Dinner · the largest meal

  • Chicken or steak with starchy and green vegetables is the most common dinner (meat and three veg).
  • Pasta with meat, such as spaghetti bolognese, is also common.
  • Dinner holds about two-thirds of the day's meat.
  • Alcohol is common with dinner for adults.
NNPAS 2011–12 (Sui, Raubenheimer & Rangan 2017)

Your observation from the shop matches the data. On any given day, one Australian in four eats a sandwich or roll and one in two drinks coffee. The ABS release has foods only, not meals. The meal-by-meal picture above comes from the 2011–12 survey, which is the newest one analysed meal by meal.

Why this happens
  • Every main gets 1–2 sides. The rule was written for meat-and-three-veg dinners. A sandwich, burger, pizza or curry with rice is already a whole meal: it has the grain, the protein and some vegetables.
  • Sides are chosen by food group only. Since m5-03 they lean toward fruit and dairy at lunch. There's no rule that pizza goes with salad and not with bamboo shoots.
  • Lunch carries 35% of the day's fruit. Real Australians eat their fruit at breakfast or as a snack. The snack slot (m5-04) is where it belongs.

4Your first question: does a Snack tab repeat the fruit in meals?

Partly, today. Fruit is already a side on most breakfast plates and many lunch plates, because the m5-03 pattern asks breakfast for 40% of the day's fruit and lunch for 35%. The snack would use the same plate engine with its own rules, so it's the same machinery, not new.

The fix is to move lunch's fruit and dairy share to the snack. Breakfast keeps its fruit, as on cereal. Lunch becomes a sandwich or a wrap, and the apple and cheese become the 3 pm snack. That matches the survey, and it also stops lunch looking like a snack platter. So the snack slot and the plate-shape fix belong together.

5What I'd change

  1. Whole dishes stand alone. Each sub-group in the meal table gets a shape:
    • whole dish: sandwiches, rolls, wraps, burgers, pizza, sushi, poke bowls, pasta dishes, curries and stir-fries with rice, soups, lasagne. Served alone, or with at most one side from a short "goes with" list (a side salad with pizza, a piece of fruit with a sandwich at breakfast).
    • needs sides: plain meat, fish, eggs, tofu, cereal. These keep 1–2 sides, with dinner shaped as protein + starchy vegetable or grain + green vegetable (the survey's most common dinner).
  2. Sides "go with" the main, like the snack pairs: dinner sides come from vegetable and starch groups that suit the main, not from whatever fills a serve gap.
  3. Fruit and dairy move from lunch to the snack, as above.
  4. Fix the wrong foods found here: dry lentils, infant pasta, evaporated milk as a drink, panettone and sweet buns as breakfast or side foods.
  5. Coffee and tea: your call (question 2).
  6. Missing dishes (bánh mì, phở): your call (question 3).

6Questions

Q1 · Whole dishes
Should a burger, sandwich, pizza or curry with rice be suggested on its own, or with at most one fitting side? Recommended: yes. It changes the m5-01 plate rules for lunch and dinner, so I'd do it before the snack build, and build both on the same "goes with" lists.
Q2 · Coffee and tea
Half of Australians drink coffee on any given day. Options:
  • (a) Keep drinks out of suggestions, as in the snack design (D3).
  • (b) Offer an optional "+ flat white" or "+ tea" on breakfast cards only, which logs with the plate if ticked. The milk in a flat white also counts toward dairy. It would be hidden during pregnancy, for caffeine.
  • (c) Treat coffee as a full suggestion on every meal.
Recommended: (b), but only after the plate shape is right.
Q3 · Missing dishes
Bánh mì and phở aren't in AUSNUT. To add them from a qualified source, we would build each from AUSNUT ingredients (roll, pork, pâté, pickled vegetables, herbs; or broth, rice noodles, beef, herbs) and label the nutrition as an estimate. Recommended: yes, but as a small separate task, starting from a list of dishes you see at work. You know that list better than any survey does.
